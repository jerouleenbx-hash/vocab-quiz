Topic vocabulary: Time
WordDefinitionExample
abrupt (adj)sudden and unexpected, often in an unpleasant wayOur friendship came to an abrupt end.
era (n)a period of time that has a particular quality or characterWe are living in an era in which technology is developing very rapidly.
millennium (n)a period of 1,000 years or the beginning of a period of 1,000 yearsPeople celebrating the millennium filled the streets.
punctual (adj)arriving or happening at the time agreed onMrs Marsh liked her guests to be punctual.
anachronism (n)something that is no longer suitable for or relevant to modern timesShe regards the marriage ceremony as a quaint anachronism.
eternal (adj)continuing for ever or for a very long timeShe has earned our eternal gratitude.
obsolete (adj)no longer used because of being replaced by something newer and more effectiveMost computer hardware rapidly becomes obsolete.
seasonal (adj)available only during a particular time of yearThe work was well paid but seasonal.
seasonal (adj)suitable or typical of the time of year it is nowWe needed to find some seasonal music.
annual (adj)happening once a yearWe've got our annual conference next month.
annual (adj)calculated or considered over a period of one yearYou'll receive an annual salary of €100,000.
expire (v)if an agreement, offer or official document expires, the period of time during which it exists or can be used comes to an endThe loan offer is due to expire this week.
overdue (adj)if something is overdue, it should have arrived/been done/been paid/etc before nowThis change in attitude is long overdue.
simultaneous (adj)happening or done at the same timeIn simultaneous announcements, the two men resigned from their jobs.
antique (adj, n)old and valuableWe offer a wide range of antique furniture.
antique (adj, n)an old object such as a piece of furniture or jewellery that is valuable because it is rare, beautiful or well madeThe desk is an antique.
frequency (n)the number of times that something happens during a period of timeChanges in sea temperature will increase the frequency of hurricanes.
period (n)an amount of time during which something happensThe long dry period ended with torrential rain.
span (v, n)the amount of time that something lastsNearly 100 witnesses testified over a span of 20 days.
span (v, n)to last for a particular period of time, especially a long periodHis career spanned half a century.
century (n)a period of 100 years, usually counted from a year ending in -00His family has ruled Morocco since the 17th century.
instantaneous (adj)immediateRecently developed medical tests can give instantaneous results.
permanent (adj)happening or existing for a long time or for all time in the futureShe suffered permanent brain damage as a result of the accident.
spell (n)a period of time, usually a short oneI had a two-week spell in hospital.
chronological (adj)arranged or described in the order in which events happenedThe paintings are presented in chronological order.
interim (n, adj)intended to last or perform an activity only until someone or something permanent or final is availableThis is only an interim report.
interim (n, adj)the time between one thing happening and another, or while waiting for something permanent or final to become availableIn the interim between John Smith dying and Tony Blair becoming leader, Margaret Beckett acted as leader of the labour Party.
phase (n)a particular period of time during the development of somethingThe war was about to enter its final phase.
stint (n)a period of time spent doing somethingHe came to California after a brief stint as a waiter in New York.
contemporary (n, adj)modern or relating to the present timeI'm not very keen on contemporary art.
contemporary (n, adj)someone alive at the same time as a particular event or personMost of his contemporaries regarded him as a mere eccentric.
interval (n)a period of time between two eventsThe normal interval between our meetings is six weeks.
interval (n)a short break between the parts of something such as a play or concertHow long is the interval?
postpone (v)to decide that something will not be done at the time when it was planned for, but at a later timeOur exam has been postponed until next Monday.
subsequent (adj)happening or coming after something elseIn subsequent interviews, Steele has contradicted his original story.
decade (n)a period of ten years, especially one beginning with a year that ends in -0Prices have risen sharply in the last decade.
lapse (v, n)a short or temporary period when you fail or forget to do things in the right wayThe man admitted driving over the speed limit and apologised for the lapse.
lapse (v, n)a period of time between two eventsThere was a lapse of ten years between his visits.
lapse (v, n)to stop gradually or for a short timeAt this point the waiter came up with Maggie's drink and conversation lapsed.
lapse (v, n)if an official document, decision or right lapses, it is no longer effectiveThe permit was extended for another year before being left to lapse.
prior (adj)happening, existing or done before a particular timeIllegally parked cars may be removed at any time without prior notice.
temporary (adj)existing, done or used for only a limited period of timeThese measures are only temporary.
duration (n)the period of time during which something continues to happen or existThe duration of the film is 110 minutes.
lifetime (n)the period of time when someone is aliveHe achieved a lot in his short lifetime.
lifetime (n)the length of time that something exists or worksThere are plans to extend the lifetime of the power station.
prompt (adj)immediate or quickPrompt action is required.
prompt (adj)happening or arriving at exactly a particular timeThe meeting got off to a prompt start at ten o'clock.
timely (adj)happening at the most suitable timeThanks to the UN's timely intervention, a crisis was avoided.
elapse (v)if time elapses, it passesAnother hour elapsed and still the wind continued to howl.
long-standing (adj)having existed for a long timeThere's a long-standing tradition in our family of giving presents on Christmas Eve.
provisional (adj)intended to be temporary, and likely to be changed when other arrangements are madeThe provisional government lasted three months.
provisional (adj)if an arrangement is provisional, the people involved have not yet said that they definitely want to do itWe've made a provisional reservation for next week.
vintage (n, adj)vintage wine is excellent in quality and was made several years agoLet's get some vintage champagne!
vintage (n, adj)a vintage object or vehicle is old, but kept in good condition because it is interesting or attractiveMy father has a number of vintage motorcycles.
vintage (n, adj)showing the best or most typical qualities of someoneShe turned in a vintage display of tennis to win the title.
vintage (n, adj)all of the wine produced in a particular year, or the year it was producedThis year's vintage will be the best in many years.
vintage (n, adj)the time when something was producedHe's got a collection of guns of all types and vintages.
Topic vocabulary: Work
WordDefinitionExample
civil service (n phr)a country's government departments and the people who work in themHow long have you worked for the civil service?
efficient (adj)someone or something that is efficient works well and produces good results by using the available time, money, supplies, etc in the most effective wayThe new machine is far more efficient than the old one.
marketing (n)the ways in which a company encourages people to buy its products by deciding on price, type of customer and advertising policyJim, come and meet the firm's new director of marketing.
recruit (v, n)a new member of a military force or an organisation, especially someone who has recently joinedShe's responsible for training new recruits.
recruit (v, n)to get someone to work in a company or join an organisationWe won't be recruiting again until next year.
recruit (v, n)to get someone to help you do somethingThe club has recruited volunteers to help.
client (n)someone who pays for or uses the services of a professional person such as a doctor or lawyerShe advises clients on their investments.
executive (n)a senior manager in a business or other organisationWe've arranged a meeting with some of the company's top executives.
multinational (n, adj)a large company that has offices, shops or factories in several countriesMy uncle works for a large multinational.
multinational (n, adj)a multinational company or business has offices, shops or factories in several countriesMy uncle works for a large multinational corporation.
redundant (adj)if someone is redundant, they have been told they must leave their job because they are no longer neededFive thousand miners were made redundant when the tin market collapsed.
colleague (n)someone who works in the same organisation or department as youFriends and colleagues will remember him with affection.
fire (v)to make someone leave their job, sometimes as a punishmentShe was fired for refusing to comply with safety regulations.
promotion (n)a move to a higher level in a company, institution or sportWe try to fill our executive positions by promotion from within.
promotion (n)the activity of encouraging or supporting somethingThe campaign is concerned with the promotion of health.
promotion (n)the process of attracting people's attention to a product or event, for example by advertisingThe government's planning to introduce a ban on the promotion of tobacco products.
sack (v)to tell someone they can no longer work at their jobHe was sacked after repeatedly arriving late at work.
consultant (n)an expert or a professional person whose job is to give help and advice on a particular subjectShe's a design consultant.
consultant (n)a senior doctor in a hospital who is an expert in a particular medical subjectShe's a consultant cardiologist.
headhunt (v)to try to persuade someone to leave their job and go to work for another companyHe was headhunted by a large electronics company.
prospects (n pl)the possibility that something will happen, especially something goodThe prospects for employment in the technology sector are especially good right now.
strike (v, n)a period of time during which people refuse to work, as a protest about pay or conditions of workA strike by transport workers was held on 11th August.
strike (v, n)to refuse to work for a period of time as a protest about your pay or conditions of workPilots were striking for a 6% salary increase.
effective (adj)someone or something that is effective works well and produces the result that was intendedHumour is often far more effective than shouting.
leave (n)a period of time away from your job or the armed forcesTony, who had been granted leave, was home for several weeks.
public/private sector (n phr)the industries and services, for example schools and hospitals, that are supported by tax money and controlled by the government of a countryThe typical member of Parliament has worked elsewhere in the public sector.
public/private sector (n phr)all the businesses, industries and services that are not owned or managed by the governmentThere is less job security in the private sector.
union (n)an organisation that represents the workers in a particular industry and tries to improve pay, conditions, etcWe encourage all employees to join a union.
Phrasal verbs
Phrasal verbDefinitionExample
crop upappear or happen suddenly or unexpectedlyA new problem cropped up during the meeting.
press ahead/on (with)continue doing something in a determined way, despite difficulties, opposition or interruptionsDespite the rain, they pressed on with the hike.
dive instart doing something in a very enthusiastic wayShe dove in and finished the project in a day.
set outstart doing or working on something in order to achieve an aimHe set out to write a novel in a month.
end upbe in a particular place or state after doing something or because of doing itIf you don’t study, you might end up failing the exam.
snow underif you are snowed under, you have too much of something to deal withI’m snowed under with work this week.
kick off (with)begin (with)The conference will kick off with a keynote speech.
take onstart to employ someone; accept some work or responsibilityThe company decided to take on two new interns.
knock offstop workingLet’s knock off early today and go for a drink.
tide overhelp someone to get to the end of a difficult period of time, especially by giving them money until they can get some moreThis loan will tide me over until I get paid.
knuckle downstart working hard, especially when you should have done this earlierIt’s time to knuckle down and finish your homework.
while awayspend time in a relaxed way when you have nothing else to doShe whiled away the afternoon reading a book.
lay offend someone’s employment, especially temporarily, because there is not enough work for themThe company had to lay off 50 employees due to the crisis.
wind downend or finish something gradually; gradually reduce work before stopping completely; relax after a period of excitement or worryThe party started to wind down after midnight.
lie aheadif something lies ahead, it is going to happen to you in the futureMany challenges lie ahead for the new team.
make upwork at different times from usual because you have not worked enough at the normal timesShe had to make up the hours she missed last week.
