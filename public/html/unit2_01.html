Topic vocabulary: Thinking
WordDefinitionExample
assess (v)Sense 1: to carefully consider a situation, person or problem in order to make a judgment
Sense 2: to calculate what something costs or is worth		Example 1: We tried to assess his suitability for the job.
Example 2: Our agent will assess the value of your property.
dilemma (n)a situation in which you have to make a difficult decisionI'm in a dilemma over whether to tell him or not.
ideology (n)a system of ideas and principles on which a political or economic theory is basedState control is a fundamental aspect of Marxist ideology.
plausible (adj)likely to be true, honest or suitableA bomb was the only plausible explanation for the crash.
assume (v)to believe that something is true, even though no one has told you or even though you have no proofEveryone accepted she was telling the truth, although in fact this was quite a lot to assume.
discriminate (v)Sense 1: to treat someone unfairly because of their religion, race or other personal features
Sense 2: to recognise the difference between things		Example 1: Employers are not allowed to discriminate on the basis of gender.
Example 2: Long-range missile attacks simply cannot discriminate between military and civilian targets.
ingenious (adj)an ingenious plan, piece of equipment, etc uses new and clever ideasThis is an ingenious device for opening bottles.
ponder (v)to think carefully about something for a long time before reaching a decisionI'm still pondering whether to tell Janine or not.
baffle (v)if a problem, someone's behaviour, etc baffles you, you cannot understand it or solve itDetectives remain baffled by these murders.
dubious (adj)Sense 1: not completely good, safe or honest
Sense 2: not sure about the truth or quality of something, or whether you should do something		Example 1: The story seemed a bit dubious to me.
Example 2: I'm very dubious about his ability to do the job.
inspiration (n)a sudden feeling of enthusiasm or a new idea that helps you to do or create somethingDreams are a rich source of inspiration for some writers.
prejudiced (adj)someone who is prejudiced has an unreasonable opinion or feeling about someone or something, especially hatred or fear of a particular group of peopleRafferty's reporting was clearly biased and prejudiced against the homeless.
biased (adj)preferring one person, thing or idea to another in a way that is unfairIt was a biased report.
estimate (v, n)Sense 1: an amount that you guess or calculate using the information available
Sense 2: a statement telling a customer how much money you will charge if they employ you to do a particular piece of work
Sense 3: to say what you think an amount or value will be, either by guessing or by using available information to calculate it		Example 1: According to official estimates, over 25% of carbon emissions come from the United States.
Example 2: The committee are currently getting estimates for repairs to the stonework.
Example 3: It's difficult to estimate the cost of making your house safe.
intuition (n)an ability to know or understand something through your feelings, rather than by considering facts or evidenceArchaeologists often use their intuition to decide where to dig.
presume (v)to think something is true because it is likely, although you cannot be certainI presume you've already ordered lunch.
concentrate (v)to give all your attention to the thing you are doingI was sleeping badly and finding it hard to concentrate.
faith (n)strong belief in or trust of someone or somethingI'm delighted to know you have such faith in me.
justify (v)to show that there is a good reason for something, especially something that other people think is wrongYou'll be expected to justify your actions.
query (v, n)Sense 1: a question that you ask because you want information or because you are not certain about something
Sense 2: to ask a question about something because you have doubts about it
Sense 3: to ask something		Example 1: We have a number of queries regarding delivery.
Example 2: It is not for us to query his decisions.
Example 3: Who would do such a thing? he queried.
consider (v)Sense 1: to think about something carefully before making a decision or developing an opinion
Sense 2: to have a particular opinion about someone or something		Example 1: She paused and considered for a moment.
Example 2: They consider it inevitable that some jobs will be lost.
gather (v)to believe that something is true, although no one has directly told you about itYou're new here, I gather.
naive (adj)a naive person lacks experience of life and tends to trust other people and believe things too easilyI was a naive 17-year-old at the time.
reckon (v)to believe that something is trueI reckon there's something wrong with him.
contemplate (v)Sense 1: to consider doing something in the future
Sense 2: to think very carefully about something for a long time		Example 1: I'm contemplating retirement next year.
Example 2: I haven't got time to sit around contemplating the meaning of life.
genius (n)Sense 1: someone who is much more intelligent or skilful than other people
Sense 2: a very high level of skill or ability		Example 1: She was a mathematical genius.
Example 2: Baking allows you to show your creative genius.
notion (n)an idea or understanding of somethingI haven't the faintest notion what you're talking about.
reflect (v)to think about something carefully and seriouslyJosie reflected on how easily she could have been killed.
cynical (adj)someone who is cynical believes that people care only about themselves and are not sincere or honest, or expects things not to be successful or usefulI know that some of you are very cynical about the proposals.
grasp (v)to understand somethingHe was finding it difficult to grasp the rules of the game.
optimistic (adj)Sense 1: someone who is optimistic is hopeful about the future and tends to expect that good things will happen
Sense 2: based on beliefs that are too confident		Example 1: She said that she was optimistic about the future of the company.
Example 2: That's a very optimistic assessment of the overall situation.
sceptical/skeptical (adj)having doubts about something that other people think is true or rightHarrison approved the plan, but Riley remained sceptical.
deduce (v)to know something as a result of considering the information or evidence that you haveFinding fossils far inland, he deduced that the area had once been covered by water.
guesswork (n)the process of trying to find the answer to something by guessing, or the answer found by using this methodIt was a theory based on pure guesswork.
paradox (n)a person, thing or situation that is strange because they have features or qualities that do not normally exist togetherWe get this apparent paradox of people migrating to an area that has very high unemployment.
speculate (v)to consider or discuss why something has happenedWe can only speculate on the reasons for his sudden resignation.
deliberate (v)to think about or discuss something very carefully, especially before you make an important decisionThe judges deliberated for an hour before choosing the winner.
hunch (n)a feeling that something is true or will happen, although you do not know any definite facts about itI've got a hunch there'll be an election soon.
pessimistic (adj)Sense 1: someone who is pessimistic is not hopeful about the future and tends to expect that bad things will happen
Sense 2: thinking that the worst thing will happen in every situation		Example 1: She said that she was pessimistic about the future of the company.
Example 2: That's a very pessimistic assessment of the overall situation.
suppose (v)to believe that something is probably true, based on your experience, your knowledge and any other information that you haveShe was about 35, Dexter supposed.
Topic vocabulary: Learning
WordDefinitionExample
academic (n, adj)Sense 1: relating to education, especially education in colleges and universities
Sense 2: good at learning things by studying
Sense 3: not relating to a real situation, and therefore not relevant
Sense 4: someone who teaches or does research at a college or university		Example 1: The book brings together several academic subjects.
Example 2: She's certainly bright, but she's not very academic.
Example 3: Given the lack of funding, any discussion of future plans was somewhat academic.
Example 4: The meeting was chaired by a leading Japanese academic.
graduate (v, n)Sense 1: someone who has a degree from a university
Sense 2: to complete your studies at a university or college, usually by getting a degree		Example 1: There aren't many careers for history graduates.
Example 2: He graduated from Yale University in 1936.
intensive (adj)involving a lot of teaching or training in a short timeIt's an intensive language course for beginners.
self-study (n)work that you do without the help of a teacherI have to do a lot of self-study on this course.
conscientious (adj)working hard and being careful to do things wellSimon is a very conscientious worker.
ignorant (adj)not knowing something that you should know or need to knowI was ignorant of the terms used in business.
knowledgeable (adj)knowing a lot about many different subjects or about one particular subjectHe's extremely knowledgeable about business and finance.
seminar (n)a meeting where a group of students discusses a subject with a teacherWe had a very interesting seminar on climate change.
cram (v)to study hard in order to learn a lot in a short time, especially for an examinationMy exams are in two weeks, so I'm cramming at the moment.
inattentive (adj)not giving much attention to someone or somethingI hate speaking in front of an inattentive audience.
lecture (v, n)Sense 1: a talk to a group of people about a particular subject, especially at a college or university
Sense 2: to give a lecture or a series of lectures		Example 1: The course will be taught through a series of lectures and seminars.
Example 2: She lectures on Greek Literature at the Sorbonne.
special needs (n phr)the particular needs of people who are physically or mentally disabledIf a student has special needs, he or she will be offered additional classes.
curriculum (n)the subjects that students study at a particular school or collegeOur mathematics curriculum is much broader now.
intellectual (n, adj)Sense 1: relating to the ability to think in an intelligent way and to understand things, especially difficult or complicated ideas and subjects
Sense 2: well educated and interested in art, science, literature, etc at an advanced level
Sense 3: someone who is well educated and interested in art, science, literature, etc at an advanced level		Example 1: Students were asked which task represented the greatest intellectual challenge.
Example 2: Though not intellectual, my mother was highly intelligent.
Example 3: Though not an intellectual, my mother was highly intelligent.
mock exam (n phr)an examination you take for practice before an important examinationWe've got our mock GCSE exams next month.
tuition (n)the work that a teacher does when they teach a particular subject, especially to one person or a small groupHe's been getting private tuition in French.
distance learning (n phr)a system in which students work at home with the help of television and radio broadcasts and send work to their teachers by post or e-mailAre you doing the course by distance learning?
intelligent (adj)good at thinking clearly and quickly, at understanding difficult ideas and subjects, and at gaining and using knowledgeSurely an intelligent person like you can deal with this?
plagiarise (v)to take someone else's work, ideas or words, and use them as if they were your ownThey accused her of plagiarising her speech.
tutorial (n)a lesson in which a small group of students discusses a subject with a tutor, especially at a university or collegeWe had a very interesting tutorial on climate change.
